# Sharing Psychology — the 8 NFX Motivations (social mode)

Distilled from NFX's "Why People Share: The Psychology Behind 'Going Viral'" (James Currier, Feb 2021, https://www.nfx.com/post/why-people-share). Used by social mode: content whose primary goal is shares/forwards/organic reach rather than direct response.

## The core equation

```
Likelihood to share = (benefit to sharer or their audience: utility, status, reputation)
                    − (cost: friction, effort, social risk of looking bad)
```

Sharing is pack-animal psychology. Before sharing anything, people unconsciously ask: **"how will sharing this make me look?"** Content spreads when sharing it does something for the *sharer's* identity or standing in their tribe — never because the creator is proud of it.

## Baseline principles (from the article)

1. Answer "how does sharing this make the sharer look?" deliberately.
2. Reduce sharing friction — every extra step or ambiguity shrinks the benefit-cost differential.
3. Best case: the content/product is *broken unless shared* (invites, comparisons, collaboration).
4. Language about the user, not about you — hand the audience the exact pithy line they'd say to a friend ("get a ride in 3 minutes," not "access an online rideshare marketplace"). Currier: this 10x's word of mouth.

## The 8 motivations

For every share-goal piece, name which of these it triggers. If the answer is "none," expect zero organic spread no matter how well it converts.

1. **Status** (the big one) — belonging, prestige, in-the-know. Sharing something exclusive or early confers status on the sharer. Caution from the article: scarcity tactics rarely work at launch without pre-existing status; scarcity works more reliably for genuinely limited things (discounts, tickets).
2. **Identity Projection** — tribalism, vindication. Sharing draws a line around who I am or what I'm against ("I told you so," outrage-sharing); also used to find one's tribe.
3. **Being Helpful** — utility, better, cheaper. People share useful things to look competent and nurturing to their tribe (early Airbnb, Salesforce). The pithy line you hand them is the multiplier.
4. **Safety** — fear, compliance, prevent harm. Threats command primal attention ("if it bleeds, it leads"; Nextdoor, Citizen). Compliance is the B2B flavor: the sharer looks like a protective, stabilizing force.
5. **Order** — organize, collect. High-conscientiousness people evangelize tools/systems that organize their world (Notion, Asana) and pull others into their protocol.
6. **Novelty** — entertainment, the new. Wundt curve: the sweet spot is new-but-familiar (the meme formula). Article's caution: hit-or-miss and fleeting — expect ~50 pieces per hit, each lasting days to weeks.
7. **Validation** — positive, happy, smart. People share results that flatter them (personality tests, scores, badges, milestones).
8. **Voyeurism** — window, schadenfreude, access. Vicarious enjoyment (food, travel, unboxings) and shared judgment (scandal, gossip) — two sides of one coin.

## Tension with conversion (derived guidance, not from the article)

A hard conversion CTA usually kills shareability — nobody gains status forwarding an ad. Tie-breaker: if the asset asks for money or a signup now, conversion wins — keep the hard CTA and make the body share-worthy. If the asset's KPI is reach/forwards, shareability wins — the ask goes share-native. When both genuinely matter, split the asset (share-worthy top, conversion close).
